Kir station: what comes next

Kir orbits at 115–126 km, inclined 8.7°. Every Kerblink shell stays below 110 km, so traffic never crosses it.

  • Fuel depot

    Dock Fuel Module 1 and keep it full with Phoenix 10 tankers. A full depot makes a crewed Niven trip possible without a bigger rocket.

  • Always crewed

    Rotate crews on the Kir Station Return Pod, with at least one Kerbal aboard at all times.

  • Science lab

    A lab module so samples from Iota and Ceti are processed in orbit.

  • Fewer, larger parts

    At 374 parts Kir is heavy to fly near. New modules use a few big parts instead of many small ones.

The Ciro system, as our astronomers see it

No probe has gone past Vesta yet. The near worlds are measured by radar and telescope; past Otho the figures are estimates, and some are probably wrong. Distances are in Gael units (GU): Gael's distance from Ciro, 14.0 Gm.

  • Grannus

    0.09 GU

    Second star

    A red companion of about half Ciro's mass, circling Ciro every few days, with three planets of its own: Taranis, Toutatis (atmosphere seen in spectra) and Cernunnos.

    Good

  • Icarus

    0.25 GU

    Planet

    Small (320 km), airless and scorched. It turns once every 213 hours, so a day lasts weeks.

    Good: radar-mapped

  • Vesta

    0.50 GU

    Planet

    The "hearth star": a dazzling white point in the evening sky, wrapped in cloud. Old almanacs list it as Thalia, an airless rock; radar puts it at about 1,140 km, nearly Gael's size, and the ground is hotter than an oven. One small icy moon, Eta.

    Visited: Pechka 1

  • Niven

    0.75 GU

    Planet

    An 800 km desert world with thin air about 75 km deep, a 6-hour day and half Gael's gravity.

    Good

  • Gael

    1.00 GU

    Home

    1,200 km, oceans and 70 km of air. Moons Iota and Ceti, both visited.

    Certain

  • Tellumo

    1.60 GU

    Planet

    A heavy water world 2,000 km across with nearly twice Gael's gravity. One tiny moon, Lili.

    Fair

  • Nodens

    2.13 GU

    Planet

    Cold, about 1,400 km, oceans and polar ice under 70 km of air, a slow 67-hour day. One moon, Belisama.

    Fair

  • Gratian

    2.80 GU

    Planet

    1,100 km with a 55 km atmosphere and a 39-hour day. One large moon, Geminus.

    Fair

  • Sucellus

    3.86 GU

    Planet

    A small grey world of about 300 km. One tiny moon, Caireen.

    Fair

  • Otho

    5.2 GU

    Gas giant

    About 7,000 km with a 5-hour day. Three moons; the largest, Augustus, has air.

    Fair

  • Epona

    7.4 GU

    Planet

    Pale and icy, roughly 1,000 km, perhaps a thin atmosphere. One moon known.

    Rough

  • Gauss

    10 GU

    Gas giant

    Estimated 6,000 km. A large moon, Catullus, that may hold air, and a second, Loki.

    Rough

  • Sirona

    14 GU

    Gas giant

    Faint rings, found when it passed in front of a star. Two moons known.

    Rough

  • Nero

    20 GU

    Gas giant

    The largest planet, perhaps 12,000 km, with at least four moons; Hadrian is larger than Iota.

    Poor

  • Hox

    30 GU

    Dwarf

    A faint world 500–800 km across; may be a double with its moon Argo.

    Poor

  • Leto

    39 GU

    Dwarf

    Found only recently by its slow drift; thought larger than Hox.

    Poor

  • Robau

    343 GU

    Distant star

    A dim star circling Ciro over thousands of years. Unseen planets suspected from its wobble.

    Speculative

The probes will settle all of it. Tianwen-1 has checked Vesta up close.

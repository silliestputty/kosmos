The next ten years

Proposal by the Lieutenant for the Director, 8 November 2026. Plans, not promises: launch windows and science will move the dates.

KOSMOS has won the race to another planet. The next race is about staying: fuel in orbit, crews that never come home all at once, and a station built for the decade ahead instead of one stretched past its limits.

Mission plan

Five phases. Each one opens with a gate: the feat or hardware the next phase depends on.

Phase 1, now

Hold the orbit

  • Dock Kir Fuel Module 1 and refuel it from a Phoenix 1 tanker: first refuelling in orbit
  • Rotate Kir's crew without leaving it empty
  • Kerblink groups 2 to 5: first complete constellation
  • Paying work: the Ceti station contract and the Gael polar satellites

Gate: fuel transferred at Kir

Phase 2

The moons

  • Chang'e-Ceti: first crew on Ceti
  • Long crew rotations at the Iota base
  • Iota Gateway: a small crewed outpost in Iota orbit

Gate: crew home from Ceti

Phase 3

The near planets

  • Niven orbiter: first probe to orbit another planet (window every 788 days, 176-day trip)
  • Zhurong: lander and rover on Niven
  • Pechka 2: a longer-lived Vesta lander, with a relay left in orbit

Gate: rover driving on Niven

Phase 4

Further out

  • Tellumo flyby and its moon Lili
  • Gratian orbiter, the farthest planet reached
  • Grannus probe to the second star's planets, on a nuclear upper stage

Gate: Guanghan depot fully operational

Phase 5

Crew on another planet

  • Crewed Niven landing, fuelled at Guanghan
  • A permanent Niven outpost

Gate: first crew on another planet

Kir today

Kir has earned its place: crewed, docked to again and again, the station the Americans still cannot match. But it was built from many small parts, and it shows.

Three ways forward

Extend Kir

Dock Fuel Module 1, add a science lab, and merge small modules into fewer large ones. Cheap and immediate, and wins the refuelling race now. It cannot fix the part count for good.

Build Guanghan, then retire Kir

A new station of a few large modules, built while Kir stays crewed. Kir hands over its crew and fuel module, then is retired.

Outposts beyond Gael

Small stations where the missions are: Iota Gateway first, a Niven station in phase 5. They complement a main station; they do not replace it.

Guanghan station

Named for the moon palace of the old stories, Guanghan replaces both Kir and the earlier Guanghan plan. It is built big, because the launch cadence can carry it: a spine of large modules under a 64 m power truss, a fuel depot at the back and a docking hub at the front. It flies at 180 km and 8.6°, the plane a due-east launch reaches directly, above Kir's traffic.

From Kir to Guanghan

  1. Kir wins the refuelling race

    Fuel Module 1 docks at Kir and takes its first tanker load. This happens first, whatever else slips.

  2. The core flies

    Research Heavier Rocketry and Large Volume Containment, fly Jinwu and the Super Heavy, then launch the Guanghan core uncrewed.

  3. Power, depot and habitats

    The truss, the Liangcang depot and the habitats dock. The first long-stay crew moves in while Kir stays crewed.

  4. Handover

    Kir's crew transfers to Guanghan. The tug carries Kir's fuel module across and docks it to the depot.

  5. Kir retires

    Kir is stripped of anything useful and deorbited over the ocean, ending the frame-rate cost for good.

Against the Americans

Their Citadel design needs eight launches for twelve crew. Guanghan takes about fifteen, but it houses fourteen, has a working fuel depot from the seventh flight and a tug and arm to keep it running. Their Reservoir depot is still a drawing; ours would already be fuelling ships for Niven.
